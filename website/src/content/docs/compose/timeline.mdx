`filmstrip-compose-ui` holds the drawn pieces of an editor, built on Compose foundation with no Material. It sits on top
of [`filmstrip-compose`](/compose/bindings/).

## FilmstripTimeline

`FilmstripTimeline` assembles a ruler, a scrolling strip, a playhead and an optional trim window.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/compose/Timeline.kt" region="timeline" />

A drag on the ruler moves the playhead and a drag on the strip moves the view. The strip follows the playhead during
playback, and stops as soon as the user scrolls it.

It doesn't need a player. `position` comes from whatever clock you run, and a timeline with no `scrub` is one where a drag
on the ruler doesn't seek.

One track is drawn. A composition with several shows the timeline they lay out on, with no clip edges marked.

## Trim

The trim window draws when you pass both `trim` and `onTrimChange`. The handles only move where `trim` next puts them, so
the range lives in your state.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/compose/Timeline.kt" region="trim" />

`setPlaybackRange` confines playback to the window while the strip keeps covering the whole source, so a drag doesn't
rebuild the composition the player is running. The trim reaches the edit when you build the one you export.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/compose/Timeline.kt" region="trim-export" />

The window keeps to `FilmstripTimelineDefaults.Trim`, a minimum of 200 milliseconds, unless you set another
`TrimConstraint`. `MinDuration`, `MinMaxDuration` and `FixedDuration` are the options.

## Options

`zoom` sets how far in the timeline is drawn, and null fits the whole composition on screen. A null zoom refits when the
width changes, like on a rotation or a window resize, until someone zooms. `TimelineZoom.of` takes a step from 0 to 10.
`TimelineOptions` holds the rest, such as sizes, `followPlayhead` and the `pinchZoom` and `wheelZoom` switches.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/compose/Timeline.kt" region="options" />

Keyboard control doesn't go through here, since it needs focus and a component that took focus on composition would steal
it from your screen. Apply `Modifier.timelineKeys` yourself, such as on an element in the `overlay` slot, where
`TimelineState` is at hand.

The `overlay` slot draws over the tiles and under the trim window and the playhead. Anything `FilmstripTimeline` doesn't lay
out the way you want, you can build from the same public parts: `FilmstripStrip`, `TimelineRuler`, `Playhead` and
`TrimOverlay`.

## VideoStage and crop

`VideoStage` is a box the shape of the output frame, holding the video and whatever you draw over it. It takes the largest
rectangle of that shape that fits, so give it room to fit into, like a `padding` inside a centred box, and not a
`fillMaxSize`.

`CropOverlay` inside a stage draws a draggable rectangle over the frame.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/compose/Timeline.kt" region="stage" />

A live player's frame wins over `outputAspect`, so the stage and the picture change shape together. Passing a null player
draws the `fallback` slot at the same size instead.

The rectangle addresses the frame under it, so the preview stays uncropped and the crop joins the edit when you export.
