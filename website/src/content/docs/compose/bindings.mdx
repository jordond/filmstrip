`filmstrip-compose` holds the Compose Multiplatform bindings: player state, a cache for frame strips and a bare video
surface. It draws no controls.

## Player and surface

`rememberFilmstripPlayer` builds a player and closes it when it leaves composition. A new composition is loaded for you,
so ordinary editing needs no debounce. `VideoSurface` renders the player and nothing else, and anything you draw after it
composites on top of the video.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/compose/Bindings.kt" region="player" />

Size the surface to the composition's output aspect, not the source's. `videoAspect` does that from
`rememberVideoSurfaceState`, and stays inert until the engine reports a size.

`contentScale` is a fit in the view. `VideoContentScale.Fit` shows bars in the view that never reach the file, unlike
letterboxing in the composition, which writes real pixels.

<Aside type="caution">
`remember` survives recomposition but not a configuration change, so a rotation tears the player down and builds a new
one. For anything longer-lived than a throwaway preview, hoist the player somewhere that outlives the screen and pass it to
`VideoSurface`, which never owns it.
</Aside>

## Playhead

`rememberPlayheadState` follows the playhead and the composition's length. It has `position`, `duration` and `progress`,
read at `PlayheadDefaults.Tick` unless you ask for another `tick`.

## Scrubbing

`rememberScrubState` holds the three calls a drag makes. Call `onScrubStart` on touch down, `onScrubTo` on every delta and
`onScrubEnd` on touch up, which settles on the exact frame. `onScrubTo` is ignored outside a gesture. A second form takes
your own `onSeek`, for a preview you run on your own clock. The [timeline](/compose/timeline/) wires this up for you.

## Events

`PlaybackEventEffect` collects a player's events for as long as it stays in composition. Use it instead of your own
`LaunchedEffect`, since `events` replays nothing and a hand-rolled collector can race the command that produced an event.

## Frames

`rememberFilmstripFrame` holds one exact frame, for a poster or a clip card. It fetches again when the position, the height
or anything that changes a rendered frame moves. The last frame stays in place while the next renders, and a failed render
clears it.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/compose/Bindings.kt" region="poster" />

`rememberFilmstripFrames` holds a whole strip. Tell it which items are showing with `onVisibleRange`, since nothing is
fetched until you do. It fetches the visible items plus an overscan either side and closes everything outside that window.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/compose/Bindings.kt" region="strip" />

Requests are served one at a time, since extraction next to a preview contends for the device's decoders. `maxBytes` caps
how much decoded pixel data it holds. An edit that doesn't change a rendered frame, like a clip's gain, keeps every frame.

<Aside type="caution">
A frame read from a strip is only valid while its index is inside the reported window. Some platforms hand out the decoded
frame itself, so drawing a bitmap you kept after scrolling away draws released pixels.
</Aside>

A frame you fetched yourself with `filmstrip.frame` or `filmstrip.frames` draws through `toImageBitmap`. It returns null
once the image is closed. Keep the image open while the bitmap is on screen and close it after, since Android draws the
image's own pixels and the other platforms copy them.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/compose/Bindings.kt" region="own-frame" />

<Aside type="note" title="Limits">
The Compose artifacts target Android, iOS, the JVM and the browser. They have no macOS target.
</Aside>
