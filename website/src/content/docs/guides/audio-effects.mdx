An audio effect is a spec on a clip, a track or the composition, and a kernel that processes samples. Android, Apple and
the browser run the same kernel. ffmpeg can't run Kotlin, so there an effect is written as ffmpeg filters.

Audio effects have their own lists and their own builder. They aren't part of `effects { }`, and changing one never
redraws a frame or a thumbnail.

## Scopes and order

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/AudioEffects.kt" region="scopes" />

- A clip's effects run on that clip's audio, in the order you added them.
- A track's effects run on each of its clips, after the clip's own.
- Both run after the clip's [speed](/guides/edits/) and before its [level and fades](/guides/audio/), so a fade takes
  the effect's own sound down with it.
- The composition's effects run on the finished mix, after every level, from the start of the timeline to its end.
  They run where no track has audio too, so an effect that makes a sound of its own, like a hiss, is heard under a
  still or a silent clip. An edit with no audio at all still gets an audio track then.

A muted clip runs no effect, and neither does a clip under a muted track or composition. The plan lists each effect it
left out on its [inert notices](/guides/export/#inert-notices), and does the same for a spec whose `isInert` is `true`.
A clip whose source has no audio track runs no clip or track effect either.

Any audio effect blocks the [stream copy](/reference/limits/#stream-copy), and the plan says which scope held it.

## Writing an effect

### The spec

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/AudioEffects.kt" region="spec" />

- `id` has to be stable. Namespace it like a package and never derive it from the class name.
- `isInert` defaults to `false`. Say `true` when the spec as configured leaves every sample alone.
- Make it a data class. Two edits are compared by value, and that's how the player tells a changed effect from an
  unchanged one.

### The kernel

A kernel processes one stream of interleaved floats in place. It's built once for each stream, with that stream's
sample rate and channel count.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/AudioEffects.kt" region="kernel" />

- `position` is the index of the block's first frame on the composition timeline, counted at the stream's sample rate.
  It's the same on every backend, in a preview and in an export, at every scope. Read your phase from it and the
  effect lands on the same samples everywhere.
- `process` runs on the audio thread during playback. Allocate what you need when the kernel is built, not in
  `process`.
- A kernel that delays its input, like a lookahead limiter, overrides `latencyFrames`. The host takes that many frames
  off the head of the stream and hands the tail back at the end, so the audio keeps its length and stays in sync.
- `reset(position)` is called when the stream jumps, like after a seek. Clear your state there.
- A kernel that throws fails the export with that error. In a preview it fails playback.

### The resolver

An `AudioEffectResolver` turns the spec into what the platform runs. On Android, Apple and the browser that's a factory
for the kernel. On the JVM it's a list of ffmpeg filters, or a factory for one when the filters depend on the stream's
sample rate or channel count.

The factory has to answer a new kernel every time it's called, since a kernel holds one stream's state.

Register the resolver when you build `Filmstrip`.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/AudioEffects.kt" region="register" />

A resolver returns `null` for a spec it doesn't know, and the next one is asked. A spec nobody claims is refused at
`plan()`. Returning `Unsupported` refuses it too, and `Verdict.Incapable.withoutUnsupported` then carries a plan with the
effect left out, which plays the audio clean.

## In the player

Changing, adding or removing an audio effect is a parameter change. The player keeps playing and the new kernel takes
over at the next block of audio, starting with no state. An effect whose spec is equal to the one before keeps its
kernel and everything the kernel has built up, so changing a level or one effect of several doesn't restart the rest.

## Backends

| Backend | A clip's effects                          | The composition's effects       | Preview                               |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Android | Your kernel, at the source's format       | Your kernel, on the mix         | Same kernels as the export            |
| Apple   | Your kernel, at the output's format       | Your kernel, at the output's    | Your kernel, on each track            |
| Browser | Your kernel, at the source's format       | Your kernel, at the output's    | Your kernel, at the page's audio rate |
| ffmpeg  | Your filters, ahead of the clip's level   | Your filters, after the mix     | No audio                              |

<Aside type="note" title="Limits">
- ffmpeg runs filters, not your kernel, so matching the two is up to your resolver. A filter that reads `t` reads
  composition time at both scopes. filmstrip puts the stream back to its length and format after your filters.
- A kernel starts with no state after a seek and after a change to its own spec, so a reverb's tail or a filter's
  history builds up again from there. That takes as long as the kernel itself remembers. filmstrip adds nothing to it.
- A preview and an export can run a kernel at different sample rates and channel counts. The browser preview mixes at
  the page's audio rate, and an Apple preview runs at each source track's own format. Build your kernel from the
  `AudioFormat` it's handed.
- On Apple, a preview of an edit with audio effects renders the audio of every clip at another speed before it plays,
  so it takes longer to load.
- On Apple, a preview runs a composition effect on each audio track by itself, since a player hands nobody its mix.
  It matches the export for an effect that's linear, with or without a sound of its own added on top, like an EQ or
  a hiss. A compressor or a limiter on the composition sounds different there than in the file.
- On Apple, a preview plays a track late by the `latencyFrames` of the kernels it runs through, because a player
  hands an effect no audio ahead of the block it plays. The export takes the latency out to the frame.
- On Apple, an export runs a clip's and a track's effects before it writes anything, into a temporary file per clip,
  so it starts later and needs the disk space for that audio uncompressed.
- In the browser, a clip with audio effects is decoded for itself, so a looping track's passes don't share one decoded
  buffer.
</Aside>
