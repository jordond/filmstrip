An edit is an `EditComposition`, built with `compositionOf { }`. It's data, so you can build it without an instance,
keep it in state, and hand the same value to `plan`, `export` and the player.

## Clips

`clip` appends to the primary track, which is the only track most edits need.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Edits.kt" region="single-clip" />

## Trims

A trim keeps part of a source. The end is always exclusive, whether you pass two durations or a range. Clips play
one after another in the order you add them.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Edits.kt" region="trimmed-clips" />

A trimmed export normally re-encodes at least the start of the clip. `snapWithin` lets the cut move back to the
nearest sync sample, up to the duration you give it, so the export can copy the streams instead.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Edits.kt" region="snap-within" />

The default of zero keeps the cut exactly where the trim put it.

## Tracks

`track` adds a layer that plays alongside the primary track. An audio-only track is mixed in with the rest, which is
how a music bed works.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Edits.kt" region="music-bed" />

Levels multiply down the scopes, composition then track then clip, and a fade multiplies whatever level it's on.
A track that carries video is refused at plan time unless it's the primary one.

## Composition audio

`audio(AudioSpec)` sets what happens to the mixed audio. `Mute` keeps a silent track, `Remove` writes no audio
track at all, `AudioOnly` drops the video, and `Volume` scales the mix.
