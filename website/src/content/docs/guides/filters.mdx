Filters are looks that stay out of the `filmstrip` bundle. They live in `filmstrip-effects-filters`, so an app that doesn't
use one doesn't carry one. See [Installation](/start/installation/#modules) for the artifact.

<PagePlayground {...frontmatter.playground} />

Adding the artifact is all it takes. Every `Filmstrip` you build can render its filters.

## VHS

`vhs` makes a frame look like it was played back from a tape. It softens luma, smears chroma along each line, wobbles the
lines, bends the bottom of the frame like a head switch, and adds streaky noise, dropouts and a tracking band. Every
backend draws the same tape, and the pattern follows the frame's composition time, so a seek or a smaller preview shows the
same picture. The look is tuned on home camcorder tapes, so it is soft and a little washed out at every speed.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="vhs" />

| Setting     | What it does                                                                                 |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `intensity` | How strong the look is. `0f` is an inert notice, unless a `settle` starts above it          |
| `tape`      | `TapeSpeed.SP`, `LP` or `EP`, from sharpest to softest. Defaults to `EP`                     |
| `seed`      | Picks the pattern of noise and events. Equal seeds draw the same tape                        |
| `tracking`  | The band of mistracked tape, `events()`, `rolling()` or `Tracking.Off`. Defaults to `events()` |
| `clock`     | `TapeClock.Composition` or `TapeClock.Wall`, what the pattern moves with in a preview         |
| `settle`    | `settle(from, over, easing)`, starts the look at another strength and eases it to `intensity`  |

`events(chance)` runs a band now and then, each one drifting down the frame and fading out over four seconds.
`rolling(period)` runs one band, rolling from top to bottom every `period` for as long as the look is drawn unless it
settles, the look of a tape that needs tracking. Both take a block that shapes the band.

A rolling band is at the top of the frame on the first frame of the scope the look is in, which is the clip's slot for a
look on a clip, each clip of the track for a look on a track, and the composition for a look on the composition, and again
every `period` after it. Pass `at`, which cannot be negative, to put it there that long after the first frame instead. `rolling(period = 6.seconds,
at = 2.seconds)` is at the top two seconds into the run and keeps rolling on the same period either side. Because it
counts from the first frame, a clip has the same band wherever it sits in the composition, and reordering or trimming it
does not move the band. A preview set to `TapeClock.Wall` draws from its own clock and ignores `at`.

A look can settle, the way a deck does when it locks onto a recording. `settle(from = 1f, over = 4.seconds)` starts
the look at the strength `from` on the first frame of its scope and eases it to `intensity` over `over`, paced by the
easing. Every figure of the look follows that one level together, the colour a tracking band takes included, so the look
on any frame is the look at the strength it has there. After the settle the look is the plain one. A preview set to
`TapeClock.Wall` draws its pattern and its band from its own clock and settles on the frame's own time, so it shows the
same settle an export does, with a band that settles already gone.

A settle counts from where a frame sits in its run, so it starts again each time a run starts. A look on a track settles
on every clip of the track, and on every lap of a looping track. Put a settle that should happen once on the clip, or
on the composition, that holds the first frame.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="vhs-settle" />

A rolling band can ease off too. Pass `settle = TrackingSettle(over, easing)` and the band's slide, noise, colour fade
and snow fall together from full strength at `at` to nothing after `over`, paced by the easing, while the band keeps
rolling. The band is at full strength before `at`, is gone once the settle has run, and does not come back, so a band
that settles over one `period` rolls once and is gone. A `TapeClock.Wall` preview draws the band gone.

| Band setting | What it does                                                                                 |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `height`     | How tall the band is, a fraction of the frame height from `0.02f` to `0.5f`. Defaults to `0.07f` |
| `shift`      | The largest sideways slide in the band, a fraction of the width up to `0.1f`. Defaults to `0.02f` |
| `snow`       | How far the band mixes toward black and white snow, from `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0.6f`    |

`intensity` scales the slide, the noise and the snow, and the chance of an event band by its square.

The pattern follows the frame's composition time, so a paused preview holds still and a seek shows the tape an export
draws there. Set `clock = TapeClock.Wall` and a preview draws the pattern from its own clock instead. The noise,
dropouts and tracking band keep moving while the preview is paused, the way a paused tape does, and the preview stops
matching the export frame for frame. An export always draws from composition time, whichever clock is set, so two
exports of one edit match. So do thumbnails and frames read out of the edit. The JVM desktop preview has no such
clock, see [limits](/reference/limits/#filters).

A `vhs` on the composition covers the whole frame, bars included. One on a clip or a track doesn't grade the bars, and
picture it moves into a bar lands over the fill. It runs under any overlay at its own scope, so a caption next to it stays
clean. A clip overlay under a composition `vhs` is part of the clip's frame by then, and is burned in with it.

## Betamax

`betamax` plays the frame back from a Betamax tape. It draws the same kinds of damage `vhs` does with more luma detail,
a quarter less noise and a quarter less colour bleed, so it reads as a cleaner tape. The difference shows beside a `vhs`
at `TapeSpeed.SP` and is plain beside the default `EP`.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="betamax" />

`tape` is `BetaSpeed.I`, `II` or `III`, from sharpest to softest, and defaults to `II`, the speed most tapes were
recorded at. A camcorder's `tape(speed)` shows them as `B1`, `B2` and `B3`. Every other setting is the one `vhs` has,
tracking, clock and a recorded `camcorder` included.

## Hi8

`hi8` plays the frame back from a Hi8 camcorder tape. Luma is sharper than on either cassette format while chroma stays
as soft, so more detail holds and colour still smears along each line. Colour sits closer to its luma, the luma grain
is a little heavier, the colour noise is lighter, and dropouts come three times as often and run half as long, the
short white ticks the format is known for.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="hi8" />

`tape` is `Hi8Speed.SP` or `LP` and defaults to `SP`. A camcorder's `tape(speed)` shows them as `SP` and `LP`. Every
other setting is the one `vhs` has.

## Tape audio

`tapeAudio` makes a clip sound like the sound track of a tape, the linear track of a VHS unless it is given another
`tape`. It narrows the band, lays hiss under the sound, and bends the pitch with a slow wow and a fast flutter. It's an [audio effect](/guides/audio-effects/), so it goes in
`audioEffects` on a clip, a track or the composition. The hiss and the bend follow the composition time, so a seek, a
second export and every clip on a track play the same tape.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="tape-audio" />

| Setting     | What it does                                                                                     |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `intensity` | How strong the sound is. `0f` leaves the audio alone and is listed as an inert notice            |
| `tape`      | A `TapeSpeed`, `BetaSpeed` or `Hi8Speed`. Defaults to `TapeSpeed.EP`                             |
| `seed`      | Picks the hiss and where the wow and flutter start. Equal seeds play the same tape               |
| `bandLimit` | How far the band narrows toward the tape's own, from `0f` for the full band to `1f`              |
| `hiss`      | How loud the hiss is against the tape's own, from `0f` for none to `1f`                          |
| `wow`       | The slow bend in pitch, a `Wobble(depth, rateHz)`                                                |
| `flutter`   | The fast bend in pitch, a `Wobble(depth, rateHz)`                                                |
| `mono`      | Folds the channels to one sound, as a linear track holds on nearly every deck. Defaults to `true` |

Each tape speed has its own figures, and a setting scales the figure rather than replacing it.

| Speed               | Band              | Hiss under full scale | Wow              | Flutter        |
| ------------------- | ----------------- | --------------------- | ---------------- | -------------- |
| `TapeSpeed.SP`      | 100 Hz to 10 kHz  | 43 dB                 | 0.15 % at 0.8 Hz | 0.10 % at 9 Hz |
| `TapeSpeed.LP`      | 100 Hz to 7 kHz   | 41 dB                 | 0.25 % at 0.8 Hz | 0.15 % at 9 Hz |
| `TapeSpeed.EP`      | 100 Hz to 5 kHz   | 39 dB                 | 0.35 % at 0.8 Hz | 0.20 % at 9 Hz |
| `BetaSpeed.I`       | 100 Hz to 11 kHz  | 44 dB                 | 0.12 % at 0.8 Hz | 0.08 % at 9 Hz |
| `BetaSpeed.II`      | 100 Hz to 7 kHz   | 41 dB                 | 0.25 % at 0.8 Hz | 0.15 % at 9 Hz |
| `BetaSpeed.III`     | 100 Hz to 5.5 kHz | 39.5 dB               | 0.32 % at 0.8 Hz | 0.18 % at 9 Hz |
| `Hi8Speed.SP`, `LP` | 100 Hz to 15 kHz  | 55 dB                 | 0.02 % at 0.8 Hz | 0.02 % at 9 Hz |

A Hi8 tape carries its sound on the picture heads, so both of its speeds sound the same and far cleaner than a linear
track.

A `Wobble` takes a `depth` from `0f` to `1f` of the tape's figure, and a `rateHz` from 0.1 to 100, or `null` for the
tape's own rate. `intensity` multiplies every setting, and at half way a corner sits half way between the full band and
the tape's on a logarithmic scale.

There is no helper that adds a tape look and a `tapeAudio` together. Declare both, and give them the same `tape` and
`seed` so they read as one recording. The defaults of `vhs` and `tapeAudio` already match.

## Film

`film` makes a frame look like it was shot on film. Halation glows warm around the brightest parts of the picture, and
grain lands over the result, heaviest in the midtones. The grain follows the frame's composition time on a fixed raster,
and the glow's width is a fraction of the frame's, so a seek or a smaller preview shows the same picture.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="film" />

| Argument   | What it does                                                                                  |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `grain`    | How strong the grain is, `0f` to `1f`                                                         |
| `halation` | How strong the glow is, `0f` to `1f`                                                          |
| `weave`    | How far the frame wanders in the gate, `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0f`                         |
| `flicker`  | How much the frame's brightness flickers, `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0f`                      |
| `dust`     | How much dust and how many scratches land on the frame, `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0f`        |
| `gate`     | How far the gate's rounded, dark edge closes in, `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0f`               |
| `leak`     | How bright the light leaks are, `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0f`                                |
| `seed`     | Picks the pattern of grain, weave, flicker, dust, scratches and leaks. Equal seeds draw the same reel |

With every argument at `0f` the look is listed as an inert notice.

`Film.Super8` sets every one of them, for small gauge stock run through a worn projector. The frame drifts a little in the
gate, its brightness flickers from frame to frame, specks of dust land for a single frame and a thin scratch runs down the
picture for up to a second and a half, a faint leak washes in from the edges, and a rounded gate darkens the corners. The gate holds still, and the rest
changes 24 times a second with the frame's composition time, so a seek or a smaller preview shows the same reel. Set the arguments yourself for a
heavier or a lighter one.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="super8" />

`leak` lets light in at the edges of the frame, the way a camera back that doesn't quite seal does. Two soft, warm
spots sit on the edges at any time. Each one opens and closes over four seconds, then moves to a new edge with a new
size and tint, and the second runs two seconds behind the first, so some light is always on the frame. The leaks sit
under the grain and the gate, and they follow the frame's composition time, so a seek or a smaller preview shows the
same leak. `seed` picks where they land.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="film-leak" />

A `film` on the composition covers the whole frame, bars included. One on a clip or a track doesn't grade the bars, and a
clear bar stays clear. It runs under any overlay at its own scope, so a caption next to it stays clean. A clip overlay
under a composition `film` is part of the clip's frame by then, and is burned in with it.

## Bloom

`bloom` makes the bright parts of a frame glow. Everything brighter than `threshold` spills soft light around itself in
its own colour, the way a lens with a mist filter on it does, so a neon sign glows its own colour and a window glows
white. The glow's width is a fraction of the frame's and it reads no clock, so a seek or a smaller preview shows the
same picture.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="bloom" />

| Argument    | What it does                                                                                  |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `intensity` | How strong the glow is, `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0.5f`                                      |
| `threshold` | How bright a part of the frame has to be to glow, `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0.7f`            |
| `spread`    | How far the glow reaches, `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0.5f`                                    |

With `intensity` at `0f` or `threshold` at `1f` nothing glows, and the look is listed as an inert notice. `Bloom.Dreamy`
is a wide, heavy glow that starts in the midtones, for a soft and hazy picture.

A `bloom` on the composition covers the whole frame, bars included. One on a clip or a track leaves a clear bar clear.
It runs under any overlay at its own scope, so a caption next to it stays clean. A clip overlay under a composition
`bloom` is part of the clip's frame by then, and glows with it.

## Digicam

`digicam` makes a frame look like it came off an early digital camera with a small sensor. Edges get the bright and dark
halo of a camera that sharpens too hard, highlights are pushed until they clip one channel at a time, and coloured noise
crawls in the shadows. The halo's width and the noise's size are fractions of the frame, and the noise follows the
frame's composition time, so a seek or a smaller preview shows the same picture.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="digicam" />

| Setting     | What it does                                                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `sharpness` | How strong the halo around edges is, `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0.6f`                |
| `noise`     | How strong the coloured noise in the shadows is, `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0.5f`    |
| `clip`      | How far highlights are pushed into clipping, `0f` to `1f`. Defaults to `0.5f`        |
| `seed`      | Picks the pattern of noise. Equal seeds draw the same noise                          |

With all three at `0f` the frame is left alone, and the look is listed as an inert notice. `Digicam.Ccd` is a cheap
compact camera pushed hard, with heavy halos, blown highlights and noisy shadows.

`stamp(recordedAt, corner)` inside the block adds the camera's orange date stamp, like `'97 6 14`, in the bottom right
unless you give it a corner. It is a [`camcorder`](#camcorder) holding one `stamp()` element, added after the `digicam`,
so the date sits clean on top of the noise and counts forward with the composition time. A `camcorder` takes `stamp()`
beside its other elements too.

A `digicam` on the composition covers the whole frame, bars included. One on a clip or a track leaves a clear bar clear.
It runs under any overlay at its own scope, so a caption next to it stays clean. A clip overlay under a composition
`digicam` is part of the clip's frame by then, and is sharpened with it.

## CRT

`crt` makes a frame look like it is showing on a curved tube. The picture bows out with black past its edge, scanlines and
a phosphor mask run over it, and the corners fall off. The scanlines and the mask are the average over each pixel, so a
smaller preview draws the larger frame's picture at its own size. Nothing reads the clock, so a seek changes nothing.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="crt" />

| Argument    | What it does                                                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `intensity` | How strong the look is. `0f` leaves the frame alone and is listed as an inert notice |

A `crt` on the composition covers the whole frame, bars included. One on a clip or a track doesn't grade the bars, and the
picture's curve ends in black while a clear bar stays clear. It runs under any overlay at its own scope, so a caption next
to it stays clean. A clip overlay under a composition `crt` is part of the clip's frame by then, and is burned in with it.

## Pixel

`pixel` makes a frame look like low resolution pixel art. The frame is cut into square cells, `cells` of them down its
height, and every pixel in a cell takes the colour at the cell's centre. A `palette` then snaps each cell to its nearest
colour, or without one `levels` cuts each channel down to that many steps. `dither` spreads the step between two colours
over a 4 by 4 ordered pattern laid on the cells. Cells are counted across the frame, so a smaller preview draws the same
cells, and nothing reads the clock.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="pixel" />

| Argument  | What it does                                                                                    |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `cells`   | How many cells run down the frame, from 8 to 1080. The columns follow from the frame's shape    |
| `levels`  | How many steps each channel keeps, from 2 to 64, or `0` to keep the colour. A palette overrides it |
| `dither`  | How strongly the ordered pattern pushes a colour toward its next step, from `0f` to `1f`         |
| `palette` | A `PixelPalette` of 2 to 16 colours, or `PixelPalette.GameBoy` and `PixelPalette.Cga`            |

`pixel` always changes the frame, so it is never listed as an inert notice. It runs under any overlay at its own scope,
so a caption next to it stays clean, and a clear bar stays clear.

## Glitch

`glitch` makes a frame look like a broken digital signal. Red and blue sit a little apart from green all the time. Now
and then a burst tears through the picture, cutting it into horizontal slices that slide sideways, with thin rows torn
further and the colours pulled wider apart. The bursts follow composition time and the frame's height as a fraction, so
a seek or a smaller preview shows the same glitch.

With `blocks` above `0f`, a burst also breaks runs of square blocks along some rows, the way a damaged digital file
does. A broken block shows a block from a few blocks away, or smears the row above it down through itself. Blocks are
sized from the frame's height, so a smaller preview breaks the same blocks at its own size. They are cut before the
slices slide, so they ride with their slice. `Glitch.Corrupt` is a strong glitch with every block setting turned up.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="glitch" />

| Argument    | What it does                                                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `intensity` | How strong the look is. `0f` leaves the frame alone and is listed as an inert notice |
| `blocks`    | How many blocks a burst breaks, from `0f` for none to `1f`                           |
| `seed`      | Picks the pattern of bursts. Equal seeds draw the same glitches                      |

A `glitch` on the composition covers the whole frame, bars included. One on a clip or a track slides a clear bar with the
picture, so the fill shows where the picture left. It runs under any overlay at its own scope, so a caption next to it
stays clean. A clip overlay under a composition `glitch` is part of the clip's frame by then, and is torn with it.

## LUT

`lut` maps a frame's colours through a colour table from a `.cube` file. Every backend reads the table trilinearly, and
`intensity` mixes the table with one that changes nothing. A table is a function of each pixel's colour alone, so a seek or
a smaller preview draws the same colours.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="lut" />

| Argument    | What it does                                                                                              |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `table`     | A `CubeLut` from `CubeLut.parse`, which takes the text of the file. Reading the file is yours             |
| `intensity` | How strong the table is. `0f` leaves the frame alone, and so does an identity table. Both are inert notices |

`CubeLut.parse` holds a grid of 2 to 129 points, applies `DOMAIN_MIN` and `DOMAIN_MAX`, and holds an entry past `0` or `1` at
the end it passed, since an SDR code stops there. It throws an `IllegalArgumentException` naming the line for a 1D table, a
size outside that range, a line it can't read, the wrong number of rows, or a file over 137 million characters.

A `lut` on the composition covers the whole frame, bars included. One on a clip or a track doesn't grade the bars, and a
clear bar stays clear. Colour effects at the same scope run before it, and filters at the same scope run in the order you
added them. It runs under any overlay at its own scope, so a caption next to it stays clean. A table authored for HDR isn't
supported.

## Camcorder

`camcorder` draws a camcorder's on screen display over the frame, like a blinking `REC`, the date and a tape counter. The
text comes from a pixel font filmstrip draws itself, white with a black edge and the record dot in red, so it looks the
same on every backend and needs no font on the device.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="camcorder" />

Each element is one call, and each takes a `corner`. Elements in the same corner stack in the order you add them.

| Element               | What it shows                                                                        | Corner        |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------- |
| `rec()`               | A record dot and `REC`, the dot blinking once a second                               | `TopLeft`     |
| `play()`, `pause()`   | A transport icon and its label. `transport(mode)` takes any `TransportMode`          | `TopLeft`     |
| `date(start, style)`  | The date, `MonthDayYear`, `DayMonthYear` or `YearMonthDay`. `start` sets `recordedAt` | `BottomLeft`  |
| `clock(style)`        | The time of day, `TwelveHour` as `AM 10:32:00` or `TwentyFourHour`                   | `BottomLeft`  |
| `counter(start, withFrames)` | A tape counter, `0:00:00`, or `0:00:00:00` with a frame count at the output rate | `TopRight` |
| `battery(level)`      | A battery with a bar for each third of `level`, blinking while low                   | `TopRight`    |
| `tape(speed)`         | The tape speed's label, like `SP`, `EP` or `B2`                                      | `TopRight`    |
| `label(text)`         | Fixed text from `A` to `Z`, `0` to `9`, space and `: . / - ' +`                      | `BottomRight` |
| `stamp()`             | A digital camera's date stamp, `'97 6 14`, in orange with no edge                    | `BottomRight` |

`recordedAt` is the date and time the date and clock show at the start of the composition, as a `RecordedAt` with no
time zone. A reading that would run past the last second of 9999 holds at `9999-12-31 23:59:59`. `textSize` is the height of a line as a fraction of the frame height, from `0.01f` to `0.2f`, and the margins
are a fraction of the frame too, so a smaller preview shows the export scaled down. A label with a character outside the
font throws an `IllegalArgumentException` naming it. A `camcorder` with no elements leaves the frame alone and is listed
as an inert notice.

The date, clock and counter read the frame's composition time, the same clock `vhs` runs on, so a seek shows the reading
that time had, and a counter on a clip still counts from the start of the composition.

### Burned in or clean

Where you declare it decides whether the tape degrades it. Inside `vhs { camcorder { } }` the display is added ahead of
the `vhs`, so it is recorded on the tape and smears and wobbles with the picture. Declared after a `vhs` at the same scope
it stays clean, like a VCR's own display over the playback. A `vhs` records one `camcorder`, and a second inside the
same block throws an `IllegalArgumentException`. `betamax` and `hi8` take a `camcorder` the same way.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="camcorder-order" />

It runs over the fill and under any overlay at its own scope.

## Night vision

`nightVision` makes a frame look like it came through an image intensifier. The frame's brightness is lifted and rolls
off toward white, heavy grain is added, and the result is drawn in phosphor green, darker toward the corners. The grain
follows the frame's composition time, so a seek or a smaller preview shows the same picture.

<Snippet file="commonMain/kotlin/dev/jordond/filmstrip/docs/guides/Filters.kt" region="night-vision" />

| Argument    | What it does                                                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `intensity` | How strong the look is. `0f` leaves the frame alone and is listed as an inert notice |
| `seed`      | Picks the pattern of grain. Equal seeds draw the same grain                          |

A `nightVision` on the composition covers the whole frame, bars included. One on a clip or a track doesn't grade the bars,
and a clear bar stays clear. It runs under any overlay at its own scope, so a caption next to it stays clean. A clip
overlay under a composition `nightVision` is part of the clip's frame by then, and is burned in with it.

## Limits

- Every filter needs SDR frames, so an HDR source tone maps to SDR first. `HdrMode.Auto` reports that as an
  `HdrToneMapped` adjustment naming the effect, and `HdrMode.KeepHdr` is refused. See [HDR](/guides/hdr/).
- On Apple, every filter apart from `camcorder` runs a Core Image Metal kernel, which takes an Apple6 GPU on iOS or
  macOS 12. A device that can't compile one refuses the filter by name.
- media3 and the browser need OpenGL ES 3, and refuse a filter by name below it.
- The browser refuses a clip whose own frame is longer on a side than the page's `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE`, since a filter pass
  draws on that whole frame.
- media3 and the browser refuse a `lut` whose table is too large for the GL context's `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE`.
- The ffmpeg backend refuses a filter by name on a build missing a filter it runs on, like `geq`, `gblur`, `lut1d` or
  `blend`.
- `vhs`, `betamax`, `hi8`, `film` and `nightVision` hand their grain and dropout planes to ffmpeg over a loopback
  socket, and draw them with `geq` at the same samples where they cannot.
- On ffmpeg, `crt` runs well under real time and holds a lot of memory per clip. A track of clips that all carry one
  equal `crt` at the output's size runs the look once after the join instead.
- On ffmpeg, `pixel` writes `yuva420p`, whose chroma is shared by 2 by 2 pixels, so a cell edge that falls inside a
  chroma sample blends the colours either side of it.
- On ffmpeg, `glitch` reads red, green and blue from their own places, so it runs in RGB and back, which rounds and
  softens the colour fringe. Broken blocks keep their luma exactly, and 4:2:0 shares one colour sample across a block's
  edge, so the colour there blends with its neighbour.
- ffmpeg lays `camcorder` from an animated PNG it writes ahead of the export, so it refuses one whose frames have no
  known end by name. It carries the display in 4:2:0, so luma lands within a code of the other backends and the record
  dot blends a little with the pixels next to it.
- ffmpeg bends `tapeAudio`'s pitch with its `vibrato` filter, so a tape with any wow or flutter is reported as an
  approximation there. A tape with neither is exact.
- `tapeAudio` holds the sound back by a few milliseconds to bend it, which every export takes out again. An Apple
  preview plays the track late by that much, see [Audio effects](/guides/audio-effects/#backends).
- `tapeAudio` starts with no history after a seek and after a change to its own settings, so its filters settle
  again over the first few milliseconds after either.
- See [Limits](/reference/limits/#filters) for the ffmpeg speed and memory figures, when the socket fallback runs, and
  how close each backend lands.
